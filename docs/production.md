# What "production" means for lexsys-hooks, and the plan to get there

*A plan with gates, written after measuring, not a promise. Effort figures are judgement and say so. Where a decision was taken on the owner's behalf so that work could start, it is marked **(default taken)** and can be reversed.*

## What the word means here

**Production** is a single-tenant, self-hosted service that an operator can run unattended for months: it does not lose an acknowledged event, one broken receiver cannot hurt delivery to another, it can be watched, backed up, upgraded and stopped safely, and it refuses to start in a configuration that is unsafe on the internet. A multi-tenant hosted service (customers, a portal, billing, SDKs, SOC 2) is a different and larger goal and is listed last, not folded in.

The README says "Not for production" today. That line comes off when the **gate of every P0 item is met and published**, not before.

## Where it stands, measured

| | |
|---|---|
| Durability | no acknowledged event lost under `kill -9` (2,000 events, `tests/chaos.py`); idempotency survives crashes |
| Capacity | about 90 to 140 µs of CPU per plain-HTTP delivery, so roughly 7 to 10k deliveries a second on one core, and about 1 ms per `https` delivery with a full handshake (0.7 ms resumed): roughly 1,000 a second; ingest 34 to 60k events a second over 64 connections (`scripts/bench/run.py`, a shared 4-core box, single runs) |
| Safety | destinations are public IPv4 literals, or names that resolve only to public addresses (judged at every attempt, section 40), unless `allow-private-hosts`; scoped tokens (ingest, read, admin) on every route and `production = 1`, which refuses to start unless the settings and the data directory are safe (section 33); one bug of this kind found by measuring and fixed (section 28) |
| **Not production** | (the logs are bounded since 0.2, below); the tokens are off until configured (`production = 1` insists on them); `https` delivery is built (section 40) on OpenSSL in the process; the authority report says `bounded: false`, and its `unbounded_by` lists 33 foreign symbols, libc's `statx` and 32 of OpenSSL (pinned in CI: `docs/authority.json`); (readiness, metrics, a graceful stop, the reason of a failed attempt and the refusal of a corrupt log are built: 0.4 and 0.5) |

## P0: blockers. Each has a gate that is a command.

**0.1 A dead endpoint must not stop the others. DONE** (design section 31; measured below). Replace the shared ring of event offsets and the single scan position (`extend_scan`, `lowmark`, `c_scanned`, `c_offset`) with a **scan position per endpoint**, so each endpoint reads the events log forward from its own cursor and is bounded only by its own window. Decide and document what a long-dead endpoint does (a circuit breaker: after N days of failures the endpoint is paused, its events wait in the log and resume on re-enable; **default taken:** paused after 5 days of consecutive failures, as the larger services do, configurable, logged, shown in `GET /endpoints`).
*Gate:* `scripts/bench/stall_probe.py` ends with the healthy endpoint at 3,000 of 3,000; a dead endpoint revived later receives all 3,000 once each, in order within its window; the whole existing suite passes; a mutant that restores the global bound fails a new test; restart in the middle of it loses and repeats nothing.

*Done, with the evidence (design section 31 has the rest):* `scripts/bench/stall_probe.py` ends at cursors **(0, 3000), (1, 0)** (it ended at (0, 1024), (1, 0)); `tests/scan_test.py` (new): the healthy endpoint is sent all 3,000 events once each beside a dead one, the dead one revived is sent all 3,000 once each and never one more than 1,024 past what it had been sent in order, twelve `kill -9` in the middle lose nothing and repeat at most what was in flight (A 31, B 42 of a limit of 96); it fails on the previous binary, and the mutant that restores the global bound fails it (A stops at 2,048); 21 of 22 mutants of the new code are killed, the survivor is equivalent in behaviour; the whole suite (23 CI steps) passes; the hot path is unchanged within noise (119 to 120 us a delivery with one endpoint, 103 to 102 with ten, four interleaved runs each) and a dead endpoint beside a healthy one costs it nothing measurable (20,000 events in 4.4 to 5.4 s against 4.0 to 5.5 s); the arrays grew by 501,200 bytes. The circuit breaker is `breaker-days` (default 5, 0 off): a run of failed attempts that has lasted that long pauses the endpoint at its next failed attempt, with the disabled mechanism and a `paused` mark, one record in the log, a line on stderr, and `GET /endpoints` and `/stats` saying so; its events wait in the log and `enable` resumes them (`tests/breaker_test.py`, 10 stages). **Not verified:** five real days (a record written five days back stands in for them), the power-cut shim in `scan_test`, a log of millions of events, the pinned compiler (the one used is two commits later, with no compiler change between).

**0.2 The logs must be bounded. DONE, with caveats** (design section 38, `docs/retention.md`; measured below). `events.seg` and `delivery.seg` were never compacted and the start read both whole. Now: events are dropped, a segment at a time, when final at every endpoint and older than a retention period (**default taken:** 30 days, `retention-days`, `0` keeps them for ever), and the outcome log is replaced by a snapshot of the state it replays to. `lexsys-log` was not changed (`docs/lexsys-log-retention.md` is the proposal for what a library would own).
*Gate:* with 10 million events through, disk use is bounded by the retention and not by history; start time is measured at 1M, 10M events and stated; a crash during compaction loses nothing (kill test at every step); `docs/design.md` says what is *not* recoverable after retention.

*Done, with the evidence (design section 38 has the rest):* **10,000,000 events** through one endpoint (before the merge with main), and **3,000,000** on the merged build, every one delivered, the data directory **between 15 and 93 MB** (the history: 3,180 MB, 34 times more) and a peak of **95 MB** (the history: 1,002 MB) respectively, resident memory flat (172 to 182 MB on the earlier compiler, 2.7 to 9.7 MB on the pinned one), no step of the loop longer than 68 ms (30 ms on the merged build), segments sealed and dropped in equal numbers, snapshots, 0 errors (`scripts/bench/retention_bench.py through`; retention 20 s, so that a month happens in seconds). **Start time:** 0.9 s at 1,000,000 retained events (2.1 to 2.2 s with 128,000 outcome records to replay; 0.7 and 1.5 to 2.1 s before the merge), **10,000,000 extrapolated, not measured** (about 7.5 to 9.5 s plus the replay). **`kill -9` as a power cut at every one of the 19 steps** of a roll, a drop and a snapshot, for both logs, by the loop and by `--compact-now`, with events final and with events pinned: 66 kills, all pass (the service kills itself at a numbered step, so the test follows the observed step and not a clock). **An event pinned by a paused endpoint, a disabled endpoint or a waiting replay is never dropped** (one test each, across a snapshot and a restart). **Keys expire as designed:** a key inside the window deduplicates across a compaction, a power cut and a restart; after it, the same key is a new event (documented); a key leaves with its window or its event; the limit is a setting (262,144 by default, 4,194,304 at most; 66,000 keys tested, restart rebuilds them in 0.06 to 0.16 s). **Ids are never reused.** **A format version** is the first record of each file and an unknown one is refused with a status of its own (30 events, 31 outcomes, 32 a hole), the directory unchanged; a directory from before is read as it was. `compact.lock` makes the online backup safe again, re-argued in `docs/runbook.md` 4.4 and tested under `kill -9` (`tests/backup_test.py` stages D and E; they found two bugs in the scripts, fixed). 30 of 34 mutants killed, the other four argued equivalent. Every test step of `ci.yml` passes, and a new one (`tests/retention_test.py`, 160 checks) was added. **Found on the way and fixed, and not retention's: a memory leak of about 11 KB per delivery attempt in the build before this work** (a lex-sys fault: a `region` left by a `return` is not given back; reproducer in `docs/lexsys-log-retention.md` gap 6), which took a service at its measured capacity out of memory in minutes; it is fixed in the two functions that every delivery used, a `memory` stage guards it, and as a side effect the snapshot of the largest state went from 0.5 s to 34 to 70 ms (design section 38.5). Hot path: no measurable change (ingest 15 us an event before and after; 71 to 81 us a delivery before, 71 to 104 after, three interleaved runs each on a shared machine).

*Caveats, stated:* the `fsync` shim stands in for a real power cut; a month is 20 s in the bench; the start at 10M retained events is an extrapolation; the first compaction of a directory with a year of history is a long burst of drops (take it with `--compact-now` while stopped); a dormant endpoint's waiting replay cannot pin its event (it is skipped, and counted); a dropped event is a `404` with no tombstone and its idempotency key is forgotten; `attempts` in PostgreSQL is not pruned; `lexsys-log` is unchanged, so its own header carries no version (`docs/lexsys-log-retention.md` is the proposal); the other places that still lose 64 KiB a call (start-up and administrative requests) are listed in design 38.5; the Docker image was not rebuilt here. Cron no longer exhausts the index in 45 days (its keys leave with their events), so the caveat under P1 "every fire uses an idempotency key" is closed by this item; the other limits there stand.

**0.3 Credentials on every route, and a profile that refuses to be unsafe. DONE** (design section 33). Before it, `POST /events`, `GET /events/:id`, `GET /events/:id/attempts`, `GET /endpoints`, `POST /endpoints/:id/enable` and `/replay` needed no credential. The plan: add scoped tokens (ingest, read, admin), and `production = 1`, which **refuses to start** unless the admin and ingest tokens are set, private destinations are off, and the data directory is not world-readable. Defaults for development are unchanged. Secrets at rest are in the clear in the table (said so since section 24.1): decide on and document either encryption at rest with a key from outside the database or an explicit statement that the database is the trust boundary.
*Gate:* a matrix test of every route against every token (a route added later without a row fails the test); `production = 1` refuses each unsafe combination with a distinct message; constant-time comparison kept; nothing in logs or stats leaks a token or a secret.

*Done, with the evidence (design section 33 has the rest):* three scopes (`ingest`: `POST /events`; `read`: the `GET`s of events, attempts, endpoints, `/stats`, `/config`; `admin`: everything that changes configuration or state, and the other two), settings `ingest-token` and `read-token` beside `admin-token` (one rule, from a file or a flag; there is no environment variable), `GET /healthz` open always (and `GET /readyz`, 0.4; `GET /metrics` is a read route), and the table of scopes in `src/authz.ls`: a route with no entry is admin-only, and `403` outright with no admin token. A scope whose token is not configured stays open (the development defaults are unchanged; the `403` "management is off" routes keep saying so). `production = 1` refuses to start, with a status and a message naming the setting for each cause: 30 no `admin-token`, 31 no `ingest-token`, 32 `allow-private-hosts` is 1, 33 the directory or `events.seg`, `delivery.seg`, `endpoints.conf` readable or writable by group or others, 34 two tokens the same, 35 a mode that cannot be read. A database is not required. Secrets at rest stay in the clear: **the database is the trust boundary**, said in the README, the runbook and the sample settings, with the advice to restrict the role; nothing is encrypted.
*Evidence (the code of this item alone, a PostgreSQL of its own, the whole suite as CI runs it then, 30 steps green):* `tests/authz_test.py` (226 checks): the routes read from the source against the rows of the test and the entries of the table, then 19 routes (21 with `/readyz` and `/metrics`) x 5 identities x 5 configurations = 475 requests (525) each with its status; no token in any of 686 answers or on stderr, and a refused token not repeated in the message that refuses it; `tests/production_test.py` (113 checks): each of the six causes a refusal of its own with its status and a message naming the setting or the path, 12 modes of the directory and 9 of each file, the umask trap, every safe combination starts, a failing `statx` is a refusal; 9 + 4 new unit tests; the comparison (`manage.authorize`, unchanged: every one of 255 positions, the length folded in) is called for all three tokens on every request; 45 mutants of the new code, 42 killed and 3 equivalent in behaviour (said why in 33.8); a route added without an entry or a row fails the matrix test (three ways), and at run time is admin-only. The hot path is unchanged within noise (about 11 us of CPU an event in every configuration).
*Remaining caveats:* the default is open until a token is set (`production = 1` is the guard); **the production profile's mode check calls `statx` through `Ffi("libc")`, because lex-sys cannot read a file's mode, and that makes `lex-sys authority` say the program calls foreign code (section 33.5); the service holds `Ffi("libc")` for that one function (the four signal functions it held for 0.4 are gone: design section 36) and the report says `bounded: false` for it and, since `https` (section 40), for 32 functions of OpenSSL (`libssl`, `libcrypto`), which go when the pure lex-sys TLS can verify chains; the `statx` call is removable in one file, and the report is bounded again when lex-sys has a file-mode builtin (lex-sys#243)**; no TLS on the service's own listener (put a proxy in front), no audit log, no rate limit on failed tokens, no rotation without a restart; a token is the only authentication, and a route's existence is visible without one (`405` against `404`); signing secrets are in the clear in the database; the container image and the unit's two changed lines were not run; extended ACLs are judged by the group bits only.

**0.4 It can be watched and stopped. DONE** (design section 34; caveats below). `GET /readyz` (200 only when the logs are open and take a write, a named database has a live connection, and the service is not stopping; otherwise 503 with the check that failed and a reason; `/healthz` is unchanged and open, and so is `/readyz`; `GET /metrics` is a read-scope route, 0.3); `GET /metrics` in the Prometheus text format (ingest accepted, duplicate and refused by status, group commits and sizes of the two logs, attempts by outcome and by reason, in flight, per-endpoint lag, cursor, disabled, paused, retries waiting and last failure, replays waiting, the history's queue and rows, the cron counters, uptime; at most 62 endpoints x 7 series); the **reason** an attempt failed (connect refused, connect timeout, no response before the deadline, reset, closed early, bad status line, `3xx`/`4xx`/`5xx`, `410`, ...) recorded as a record of kind 14 in `delivery.seg` next to the outcome **and** in `attempts.reason`, shown in `GET /events/:id/attempts` and counted in `/metrics`; `SIGTERM` and `SIGINT` stop taking requests and starting attempts, let the attempts on the wire finish for at most `stop-deadline-ms` (5000), flush, close the logs and exit 0; a second signal ends the process at once.
*Gate:* each metric is checked against a known workload; `kill -TERM` under load loses and duplicates nothing beyond the at-least-once contract and exits 0 within the deadline; the failure reason is in the log and the history table.

*Done, with the evidence (design section 34 has the rest):* `tests/metrics_test.py` (60 checks): 43 events, three endpoints (ok, 500, nothing listening), five refusals: ingest accepted 43 / duplicate 3 / refused 5 by status, group commits of the events log 43 (= the events), log sizes equal the files, attempts by outcome 43 / 172 / 86 equal to `/stats` and to the outcome records read with another reader, failures by reason 129 `status_5xx` + 129 `connect_refused` equal to the kind-14 records, cursors, lag and retries waiting right (also with a retry a minute away, and after a restart), 62 endpoints answer in 24.9 KB, the history's counters equal the rows of the table. `tests/reason_test.py` (36): ten receivers (refused port, silent beyond the deadline, reset mid-response, closes without a word, garbage, 500, 404, 301, 410, a blackholed address, and a 204), each reason in `/attempts`, `/metrics`, the table's column and the log, the coarse `status` unchanged, a replay's reason told from a window's, the last failure read back after a restart. `tests/ready_test.py` (28): a database that is cut (503 `database`; delivery unaffected), a tmpfs that fills through the service (503 `events_log`, `/healthz` still 200, a restart finds every acknowledged event) and by another process (503 `data_dir`, and 200 again with no restart). `tests/stop_test.py` (54): SIGTERM and SIGINT idle; with 8 attempts on the wire (no ninth is ever started, the 8 are recorded, exit 0, the rest delivered by the next start with none repeated); the deadline; a second signal kills at once (three combinations); **under load (four clients, a slow receiver) exit 0 in under 5.5 s, every acknowledged event in the log, and across the stop and the next start every event reached each receiver exactly once**. Unit tests: `ops_test.ls` 13 (12 once `/metrics` took its scope from `src/authz.ls`), `reason_test.ls` 6. The whole suite passed on the code of this item alone (design 34.8); the hot path is within noise (85 to 91 us a delivery, 11 to 12 us an event on ingest); mutants: 39 mutants of the new code, 38 killed, 1 equivalent (design 34.7).
*Caveats:* **(1) the authority report.** The service stopped using `Ffi("libc")` for signals when lex-sys gained a signals capability (design section 36): it now claims `INT,TERM` (`signals("INT,TERM")` in the report), the claim is watched in the event loop's poller so a stop is noticed at once, and the report no longer lists `sigblock`, `sigsetmask`, `sigpending` and `signal`. It still says `bounded: false` because of the `statx` of 0.3 (section 33.5) and nothing else (`unbounded_by: ["libc:statx"]`), and it is pinned by CI (`docs/authority.json`, `scripts/check-authority.sh`). **(2) The listening socket stays open during the drain** (new connections get `503` and are closed): the `http.server` package owns the listener and offers no way to stop accepting. **(3) `/readyz` stayed 503 (`database`) after the database returned until the service was restarted, and the start waited while the database was silent: closed** (design section 37). The pool of `lexsys-pg` reconnects without waiting; the service remakes a lost connection by itself, `/readyz` is 200 again with no restart, and the start listens at once and reads its endpoints when the database answers (`tests/pgre_test.py`). `database` is now the one `503` a restart does not repair, so the container's health check (`/readyz`) should route traffic and not restart. **(4)** `send_timeout` has no test (a request fits the socket buffers) and no attempt can end `ssrf-refused` (no destination check happens per attempt yet). **(5)** Not run: a real systemd, the container image rebuilt with the new health check, macOS.

**0.5 Refuse corruption instead of repairing it silently. DONE** (design section 34.5; found by the packaging slice, `docs/runbook.md` 4.7). (a) A `delivery.seg` that refers to an event id above the last in `events.seg` is **refused at start with status 18** and a message (it used to start, acknowledge events with `202` and never deliver them); (b) a log with damage in the middle is **refused with status 19**, saying where the log is whole to, how many bytes cutting would lose and how many intact records are among them (it used to be cut silently: 500 events became 250); `--repair-logs 1` is the explicit opt-in: it copies the bytes to `<log>.cut-<offset>`, cuts, and reports the cut. A torn tail (nothing after the last whole record validates, and it does not parse as two records) is still cut, and now said after `listening`. The check is in the service, before anything is changed, with `lexsys-log`'s own scanner: **no change to `lexsys-log` was needed**. `/readyz` reports a broken log. 
*Gate:* a test per hazard (the restored-pair and the flipped-byte cases of `tests/backup_test.py` become refusals); a torn tail of one record still recovers silently-but-logged.
*Done, with the evidence:* `tests/corrupt_test.py` (113 checks): the pair in four forms (status 18, both files byte for byte as they were, nothing listening), exactly the last event starts; seven damages (a flipped byte in the middle of `events.seg`, in a length field, in `delivery.seg`, a hole of zeros with records after it, the last three records spoiled, the second last, the first): status 19 with the numbers worked out from the bytes, files untouched; eight torn tails recovered and said, the next id continues, the next start silent; `--repair-logs` (the cut kept byte for byte, the report, refused when the file exists, a repair that would make the pair disagree is refused with nothing cut, the settings file); and **153 corruptions (150 random, 3 directed) classified by `scripts/logcheck.py` and then given to the service, which agrees on every one** (the three kinds all occur). `tests/backup_test.py` stage C item 9 (was information) is now two checks: the refused pair and the flipped byte given to the service itself. `chaos.py` (105 kills as power cuts), `delivery.py` (101), `scan_test`, `backup_test` stage B and `schedules_test` restart after cuts and none was refused. A start costs no more: a 241 MB log of a million events takes 4.8 to 5.2 s (4.7 to 5.5 s before).
*Caveats:* the rule cannot tell a crash that persisted a later page of the unsynced tail but not an earlier one (intact records after a hole) from bit rot and refuses it (`--repair-logs 1` then loses nothing acknowledged); a flipped byte inside the **last** record looks torn and is cut; **repairing damage in `events.seg` while `delivery.seg` records deliveries of the lost events is refused (18) and needs `delivery.seg` moved aside** (every event that is left is then delivered again); `lexsys-log` still cuts whatever it is handed (the service hands it only a judged log).

## P1: what users expect, in this order

1. **Event-type filtering** per endpoint (the column that section 25 left out): only the events an endpoint subscribes to are sent, decided cheaply from the stored record (the gateway design, `docs/inbound-gateway.md` D10 and D11, has the mechanism).
   **Done** (`docs/design.md` section 35). `"types": ["invoice.paid", "user.*", "*"]` on `POST` and `PATCH /endpoints`, `types=` in `endpoints.conf`, a `types` column; the type is the body's `"type"`, read once at ingest and stored as a `typ` pair of the record (a log from before has none and is read as it was; an event with no type goes only to an endpoint with no list or `*`). An event an endpoint does not want is final for it at once, in memory, at the one place it first looks at the event: no attempt, no record, no history row, the cursor slides over it, `/stats` counts it (`filtered`).
   *Evidence (final code, a PostgreSQL of its own, the whole suite as CI ran it before the merge with 0.3 and 0.4, 30 steps; after it, 37 test steps, section 35.10):* `tests/filter_test.py` (62 checks): the rules against a model written apart (exact, `prefix.*`, `*`, untyped, 128/129 bytes, a control character, a JSON escape); **3,000 events almost none wanted: both cursors at 3,000 past the window of 1,024, attempts equal to the wanted ones**; a dead endpoint on a rare type passes over 2,500 events with no attempt, holds at the rare one and passes over exactly 1,023 behind it; **39 to 40 `kill -9` driven by the receivers' progress: every wanted event at least once, none unwanted ever, cursors at the end**; a log written with the old record shapes read as before, its idempotency keys still answering; a narrowed list and a restart; replay (all: those that subscribe; one named: whatever it lists). Hot path: no visible cost (CPU a delivery 84 to 85 us with one endpoint, 69 to 72 with ten, alternated runs on a loaded machine; a pair passed over costs about 2.4 us against 70 for a delivery; section 35.8).
   *Not done:* the sparse `advance` record of D10 (a restart re-decides the events above an endpoint's cursor, under the list then in force); a log written by this version is refused by the previous binary (status 16); the type of an inbound event (the gateway).
2. **Retry jitter**, and a per-endpoint **concurrency and rate limit**.
   **Done** (`docs/design.md` section 39). `retry-jitter` (percent, 0 to 50, **default 10**, symmetric; 0 is the schedule exactly) moves every retry delay, a replay's too, by a fixed function of the endpoint, the event and the attempt, applied where the time of the next attempt is computed and written to the log, so a restart keeps it. `endpoint-concurrency` (1 to 8, default 8) and `endpoint-rate` (attempts started a second, default 0 = no limit) for all endpoints, `"concurrency"` and `"rate"` of its own on `POST` and `PATCH /endpoints` (two columns, `endpoints.conf` words, shown by `GET /endpoints`); the rate is a token bucket in the loop on the clock it already has, and **a held-back event waits, it does not fail** (no attempt, no record, no retry counted), with `hooks_endpoint_throttled_total`.
   *Evidence (final code, a PostgreSQL of its own, the whole suite as CI runs it, 39 harness steps and 197 unit tests, as root and again as `nobody`):* `tests/jitter_test.py` (21 checks): 400 events failing at one instant, delay 2,000 ms at 20 percent, every recorded delay within 1,600 to 2,400, mean within 2 percent, all ten tenths of the range occupied, the second attempt at the recorded time and never before it; `--retry-jitter 0` all 200 delays 2,000 ms (and `tests/retry_test.py`, with that flag and nothing else changed, passes); the same endpoint and events give the same delays in two services; **20 events waiting through two `kill -9`, the last restart with `--retry-jitter 0`: the recorded times are unchanged and every retry arrives at its time**. `tests/limits_test.py` (92 checks): **never more than N in flight observed at a receiver** (2, 4, 1, 3 after a `PATCH`, 8 by default and not 9), **the rate observed in every window of one second** (60 events at 10 a second: 5.9 s within 15 percent, no second above 12; 9 a second reached; 20 replays at 5), no attempt failed or repeated by being held back, **a limited endpoint at 2 a second beside an unlimited one that gets 300 events in 0.4 s**, six `kill -9` while 120 events were held back, every wrong value a 400, the table, `endpoints.conf`, the import, and `psql -f sql/schema.sql` over a table made by the old definition. 11 + 8 unit tests of the bucket and the spread. The hot path is unchanged within noise (92 us a delivery against 90.5, four alternated runs).
   *Not done:* the bucket is memory, so a restart can exceed a limit by one bucket (a tenth of a second); "unlimited for this endpoint" under a service limit is a rate of 100,000.
3. **Secret rotation with two signatures** (Standard Webhooks allows `v1,a v1,b`): the old key stays valid for a stated period; a test with the reference library's multi-signature verification.
   **Done** (section 35). `PATCH /endpoints/:id` with a new `secret` (or `rotate: true`) and `keep_old_ms` (to 30 days) or `keep_old: true` (the setting `rotation-grace-ms`, default 24 h): while it lasts every delivery carries `v1,<new> v1,<old>`; `{"keep_old_ms": 0}` ends it, a period alone moves it; two columns (`secret_old`, `secret_old_until`) and `old=<secret>@<until>` in `endpoints.conf`; survives a restart.
   *Evidence:* `tests/rotation_test.py` (61 checks) with the reference library `standardwebhooks`: a receiver that knows only the old secret, one that knows only the new, both verify during the period and a third does not; the header is exactly the two signatures the library computes, new first; after the period (observed through `GET`) only the new one verifies; a retry after the period carries one; `kill -9` in the period, an expired row, a period ended while the service was down; replay; every wrong period a 400.
4. **Dead letters you can list and replay in bulk**: `GET /endpoints/:id/dead`, `POST /endpoints/:id/replay-dead`.
   **Done** (section 39; with it the "no way to cancel a waiting replay" of section 23). `GET /endpoints/:id/dead` (read scope: event, type, attempts, reason, time of death, whether a replay waits; newest or oldest first, `limit`, `after` as an event-id cursor), `POST /endpoints/:id/replay-dead` (admin: optional `limit`, `types`, `after`; **a call takes as many dead letters as the 32 waiting replays have room for and says `taken`, `remaining`, `waiting`: the caller calls again, nothing is dropped**), `DELETE /events/:id/replay/:endpoint` and `DELETE /endpoints/:id/replays` (admin: a record, flushed before the answer; an attempt on the wire is not cancelled). The dead letters of an endpoint are kept in a table of its newest 2,048, built from `delivery.seg` at the start (a dead record now carries the time of death in its fifth field; kind 16 is a cancelled replay, kind 17 a dead letter in a snapshot) and completed from the log when room appears.
   *Evidence:* `tests/dead_test.py` (99 checks): the list of 40 against what failed and against the delivery log read by a separate reader, pages of 7 in both orders with none twice or missing and a cursor that holds while three more die, **300 events dying across three `kill -9` give exactly the 300 and the same list after another kill**, a `410`, **100 dead letters replayed by a loop of calls (none above 32, `waiting` never above 32, each sent once, list and log empty)**, `types`, `after`, `limit`, thirteen wrong bodies, a replay that dies again, **six `kill -9` in the middle of a bulk replay of 200: all sent, repeats at most what was in flight**, **2,200 dead at one endpoint: the newest 2,048 listed, `complete_above` 152, the 152 listed and replayed after the others are**, a log written by the previous binary read with `died_at` 0 (checked with the real binary of `origin/main` as well), the routes answering 503 and the list whole once the endpoints are read when the database is away at the start, and a snapshot of the outcomes log (retention) that keeps the list, the floor and a cancelled replay. `tests/cancel_test.py` (64 checks): one and all cancelled, **`kill -9` and a power cut (the `fsync` shim, the log cut to the last `fsync`) after an acknowledged cancel or bulk replay: unchanged**, an attempt on the wire `409`/`busy`, the bound of 32 with cancel-all freeing it. 9 + 4 unit tests (a model test of the full table found a real bug in the first version). 48 mutants of the new code, 47 killed and one equivalent in behaviour (39.7). **Bugs found by the tests:** the full table's shift, and a fold of the log that could not bring back what it had evicted (39.9).
   *Not done:* the list holds 2,048 an endpoint (older ones are completed from the log when there is room, at the cost of reading it); the places of the events for the types are found by one pass over the events log after a start (7.7 us an event).
5. **Custom headers** per endpoint (never the signature or hop-by-hop ones).
   **Done** (section 35). `"headers": {...}` on `POST` and `PATCH /endpoints` (the whole set is replaced), `headers=` in `endpoints.conf`, a `headers` column; up to 8, a name of 64 and a value of 512 characters, 2,048 bytes as sent; sent on every attempt and replay after the `webhook-*` headers; **`GET` shows the names and never a value**; `webhook-id`, `webhook-timestamp`, `webhook-signature`, `host`, `content-length`, `content-type`, `connection`, `transfer-encoding` and the other connection headers are refused in any case with a reason, and so is any CR, LF or NUL, however written.
   *Evidence:* `tests/headers_test.py` (87 checks): the headers at a real receiver, name and value byte for byte; 120 requests with a forbidden name refused; 20 injection attempts refused at `PATCH` and `POST`, the same bytes raw in the JSON, 12 hand-written table rows refused at start; no value in any read, answer, history row or stderr line; the largest event (65,487 bytes) with the largest headers and two signatures delivered (the attempt slot's request buffer grew 4 KiB for it).
6. **More than 62 endpoints** (the limit comes from the disabled bit-set and the per-slot window: 62 x 1,024 x 3 integers). Raise it to a stated number with the memory cost measured; the layout is a chain of computed offsets, so this is bounded work.
7. **`https` delivery.** The spike (`lex-sys` `docs/tls-nonblocking.md`) measured about 0.65 ms of CPU per verified handshake on one core, a resolver that does not block the loop, and certificate failures each with their own code. Three slices (T1 the TLS phase, T2 names and the SSRF rule moving to every attempt, T3 sessions). **(default taken)** OpenSSL in-process, and the gap this exposes in lex-sys (the `Ffi` scope is only a label, so the authority report turns `UNBOUNDED`) is fixed in lex-sys; the pure-lex-sys TLS epic (lex-sys#197) replaces OpenSSL later and removes the problem.
   **Done** (`docs/design.md` section 40). T1: an endpoint whose host is `https://name` is delivered to over TLS 1.2 or 1.3 on the same poller (OpenSSL over memory BIOs, 32 functions declared under their own library), the chain and the host name verified against the system's trust store or exactly the `tls-ca-file`, SNI, a distinct reason for every failure (`cert_untrusted`, `cert_expired`, `cert_hostname`, `cert_invalid`, `tls_handshake`, `tls_timeout`, `tls_error`), retried on the schedule and dead after it; a trust store that cannot be loaded is status 21. T2: host names, resolved by the service itself (DNS over TCP on the poller, one name server, `dns-server` or `/etc/resolv.conf`; no thread, so no meeting with the signals claim), **every address of the answer judged by the ranges of section 26 at every attempt and the connection made to the address that was judged**: `ssrf_refused` (19) is now a way an attempt ends; at the write a name only has to be a name; `localhost` is the loopback; no IPv6, no `/etc/hosts`. T3: one TLS session per endpoint, in memory, resumed, dropped when the endpoint changes, `tls-resume`; `/metrics` has `hooks_tls_handshakes_total`. The scheme is part of the stored host (`https://name`): no column, an older binary refuses the line instead of delivering in clear. The authority: 37 pairs, pinned by `scripts/check-authority.sh`.
   *Evidence (final code, a PostgreSQL of its own, the suite as CI runs it, as root and as `nobody`):* `tests/https_test.py` (94 checks: every certificate failure, the handshake, partial and refused I/O, `kill -9` in a handshake, 64 held handshakes hold nothing), `tests/names_test.py` (37: 21 unsafe addresses and 4 mixed answers refused with the receiver never connected to, rebinding, one lookup per attempt, DNS failures, a slow name server, 64 lookups of 300 ms in 0.34 s), `tests/sessions_test.py` (32: resumption seen from the receiver, dropped on every kind of change, no leaks), `tests/https_api_test.py` (57: the scheme over the API, the file, the table, a backup and a restore), the unit tests (a million damaged DNS answers), and the changed assertions of the existing tests, each marked CHANGED. 49 mutants, 47 killed, 1 survivor that is redundant by design and 1 the checker refuses to compile (`tests/mutants/https.py`, `scripts/mutate.py`). Measured (`scripts/bench/https_cost.py`): a name +70 µs, a full handshake +0.9 ms, resumed +0.5 ms, plain HTTP unchanged; the loop's longest wait with 64 handshakes or 64 lookups pending 0.6 to 2.2 ms.
   *Not done:* connection reuse between deliveries; revocation; a client certificate; IPv6; `/etc/hosts`; UDP and a second name server; a cache of lookups; a per-name allow-list for private addresses; an address with an IP-SAN certificate; a real public chain was not exercised (no outbound access but a proxy); the container image and the unit under systemd were not run; the pure lex-sys TLS (epic #197) replaces OpenSSL later.

8. **Cron**: a schedule fires an event. **(default taken)** A `schedules` table in PostgreSQL (id, cron expression of five fields, event type and body, time zone UTC), a tick in the loop, and **exactly-once firing by construction**: each fire is appended as an ordinary event with the idempotency key `cron:<id>:<scheduled-second>`, so a crash and a restart cannot fire a second copy. Missed fires after a stop: fire once for the missed window and skip the rest (configurable). CRUD under the admin token, `GET /schedules/:id` showing the next fire. Effort: small, because the engine already exists (a few days).
   **Done** (`docs/design.md` section 32). `POST /schedules`, `GET /schedules`, `GET /schedules/:id` (with `next_fire`), `PATCH /schedules/:id`, `DELETE /schedules/:id`, all behind the admin token (the reads too); `src/cron.ls` (pure: parse, `next_after`, `prev_upto`) and `src/sched.ls`; the tick is requests on the existing pool, never a wait. Settings: `cron-catchup` (default 1) and `cron-seconds` (a test mode: a leading seconds field).
   *Evidence (final code, a PostgreSQL of its own, the whole suite as CI runs it, 24 steps green):* 18 unit tests plus `tests/cron_test.py` against `datetime` and a day scan (7,123 checks, 0 failures); `tests/schedules_test.py` (171 checks): a fire is an ordinary event delivered and signed; missed fires after an hour's stop are one event (not 60, not 720); the next real minute fires on time; **`kill -9` between the flush of the event and the update that tells the database, six rounds, half as power cuts, one longer than the grace period: no scheduled second has two events**; 37 power-cut kills under a schedule of every second, 25 within milliseconds of an event reaching the log: one event per second, none missing; the full idempotency index refuses a fire rather than make it without its key; every refusal is a `400` with its reason; the token on all five routes; 18 mutants, 18 killed (design section 32.8).
   *Not done or not covered:* every fire uses an idempotency key, and since P0.2 the index forgets a key with its event (design section 38), so a schedule of every minute no longer uses the index up in 45 days; one service per database; UTC only; at most 64 schedules; the CAS against a concurrent `PATCH` and the tick's own 5 s deadline are not tested; `examples {}` blocks do not exist in lex-sys (section 32.9).

*Gate for each:* its own test file, mutants, the README and the page. For 1, 3 and 5: 40 mutants of the new code, 38 killed and 2 that survive as redundancy (section 35.8).
